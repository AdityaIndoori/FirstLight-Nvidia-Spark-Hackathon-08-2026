How this deck is built — and why

Eight slides, one file, zero network requests. Every design choice below is a cited finding, not a preference.

  • Assertion-Evidence headlines. Every slide's headline is a full sentence stating its takeaway; the body is visual evidence, never bullets. Read the 8 headlines alone and you have the pitch. Garner & Alley, Int. J. Eng. Ed. 29(6) — comprehension and recall gains at p<.01 with identical spoken words.
  • ≤4 competing elements per slide. Cowan 2001: working memory holds ~4±1 chunks without rehearsal. A 6-bullet list already exceeds it while the audience is also listening to you.
  • On-screen words are not the script. The script lives in the speaker notes (press N). Mayer's redundancy principle, d≈0.86: narration + graphic beats narration + graphic + the same words on screen, because the audience reads faster than you speak.
  • Progressive reveal, forward-only. Each slide lands in 2–3 steps. Mayer's segmenting principle, d≈0.8.
  • One accent colour per slide, on the thing being spoken about. Mayer's signaling principle. Labels sit on the diagram, never in a legend — spatial contiguity.
  • Slide 2 → slide 4 is the same four-box chain twice. Red flaw becomes green answer, same geometry, same scenario. Judge testimony (OpenAI, JetBrains hackathon): "show what used to be frustrating, show the new version, make the contrast obvious" beats a feature tour.
  • One named county, one real number. Yancey County's 20-of-22 towers, from the FCC's own outage report — not a market statistic. Identifiable-victim effect, Small & Loewenstein: adding aggregate statistics to an identified case measurably weakens it.
  • We name VSS and CLARKE ourselves, and the limitation after the strengths. Stealing thunder (Williams et al. 1993) and the blemishing effect (Ein-Gar, Shiv & Tormala, JCR 2012): a small negative placed after positives raises credibility above all-positive messaging.
  • A power grid, not a 2×2. Blank cells where a rival lacks a capability, not ✗. Dreamit; and Hunter Walk: "I've never been presented a 2×2 where the startup isn't in the upper right."
  • The demo is the centre, pre-committed and judge-driven. Demo is a first-class scored criterion in NVIDIA's own Devpost rubric; a judge panel reported nearly half of pitches ran out of time before showing anything working.
  • Contrast and size floors. Body text ≥4.5:1 against background (WCAG 1.4.3); nothing below ~24 pt equivalent; the headline sits at the top where the back row can see it.
